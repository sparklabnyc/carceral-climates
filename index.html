Data and solutions for the impacts of climate-related extremes on incarcerated people in the United States

A 36-month research programme bringing together academic, formerly incarcerated, community, policy, legal and journalism partners.

Overview

Nearly 2 million people are incarcerated in the United States, most in facilities without universal air conditioning and with poor ventilation. Incarcerated people face growing exposure to heat, wildfires, tropical cyclones, floods and poor water quality.

  • Most states

    US states

    have no policy mandating universal air conditioning in prisons

  • About 2 in 3

    US states

    state Departments of Corrections do not publicly disclose emergency plans for crises such as wildfires and hurricanes

Who is at risk

  • An increasingly older population
  • Disproportionately people of colour
  • High rates of chronic illness
  • Unable to change their surroundings to reduce harm
  • Little research on how climate extremes affect their health

What we do

  • Evidence collection measures exposure, health impacts and what protects people
  • Engagement and policy turns that evidence into policy and public action

Who we work with

  • Our community partner Fight Toxic Prisons
  • Formerly incarcerated people and community organisers
  • Lawyers and legislators
  • Journalists, with our media partner the Columbia Journalism School

Evidence collection

Activities 1 to 3 generate new evidence through exposure assessment, epidemiology and intervention studies.

  1. 1

    National exposure assessment

    How does historical and projected exposure to climate-related extremes vary across US carceral facilities?

  2. 2

    Climate extremes and mortality

    What are the associations between climate-related extremes and cause-specific mortality in US carceral facilities?

  3. 3

    Mitigation and intervention assessment

    How well do climate mitigation measures, such as air conditioning, protect the health of incarcerated people?

Engagement and policy

Activities 4 to 6 focus on community engagement, public tools and dissemination, turning the evidence into policy and public action.

  1. 4

    Community engagement

    Focus groups with formerly incarcerated people, community organisers, legal experts and legislators to identify how the findings can drive policy change at every scale.

  2. 5

    Toxic Prisons Mapping Project

    Informing and updating the map to share results and support real-time disaster response.

  3. 6

    Dissemination

    Sharing results and the direct experiences of currently and formerly incarcerated people through publications, media and public events.

Toxic Prisons Mapping Project

A participatory research initiative, built with Fight Toxic Prisons, that maps the environmental hazards facing prisons, jails and detention centres across the United States. Our work informs and updates it with new evidence on climate-related extremes and health.

Hazards

Active and forecast environmental hazards from public real-time sources

Insights

Facility-level data and environmental indicators, updated with our exposure and health findings

Storytelling

Narratives from people with lived experience of incarceration and their loved ones

Dissemination

We share our findings in two complementary ways: media stories developed collaboratively with journalists, and policy briefs for decision-makers.

Media stories

  • Developed collaboratively with journalism faculty and students at the Columbia Journalism School
  • News stories, features, podcasts, oral-history-style narratives and animations
  • A collaborative class developing story ideas on climate change, public health and incarceration

Policy briefs

  • Built on our evidence, best practice and community engagement
  • Recommendations for federal, state and local decision-makers
  • An annual report for legislators and a national conference to share results
